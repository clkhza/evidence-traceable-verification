# Annotation Rubric

## Purpose

This rubric defines the six dimensions used to audit evidence traceability in AVeriTeC fact-checking records.

The central question is not simply whether a verdict is correct, but whether the record makes the evidential basis for that verdict sufficiently visible and inspectable.

## 1. Claim-component coverage (CC)

**Question:** Does the record address the relevant components of the claim?

Assess whether the evidence and explanation cover the material parts of the claim rather than only a convenient fragment.

Examples of components include:
- Actors or entities
- Actions or events
- Quantities and measurements
- Dates and time periods
- Locations
- Causal or comparative relationships

A record may cite a relevant source but still have incomplete claim coverage.

## 2. Counterevidence addressed (CE)

**Question:** Does the record acknowledge and address evidence that challenges its conclusion?

Assess whether potentially contradictory or qualifying evidence is visible and considered.

A source list alone does not demonstrate that counterevidence has been addressed. The record should make the relationship between the counterevidence and the assessment identifiable.

## 3. Alternative explanations (AE)

**Question:** Does the record consider plausible alternative interpretations or explanations?

Assess whether reasonable alternatives that could change the interpretation of the claim are acknowledged.

This dimension concerns relevant alternatives, not the obligation to list every imaginable possibility.

## 4. Context and scope (CS)

**Question:** Does the record provide sufficient context to interpret the claim and evidence?

Consider:
- Time period
- Geographic scope
- Population or entities covered
- Definitions and measurement boundaries
- Distinctions between plans, approvals, allocations, and actual outcomes

A source may be authentic and relevant while still referring to a different time period or scope.

## 5. Uncertainty acknowledged (UA)

**Question:** Does the record make material uncertainty and limitations visible?

Assess whether unresolved questions, incomplete evidence, ambiguous wording, or limitations are acknowledged where relevant.

A confident verdict does not itself demonstrate that uncertainty has been adequately considered.

## 6. Justification-to-evidence linkage (JL)

**Question:** Can a reader trace how the cited evidence supports the assessment?

Assess whether the record connects specific evidence to specific parts of its reasoning.

A citation or URL alone is insufficient. The relationship between the evidence and the conclusion should be identifiable.

## Coding scheme

Apply one code per dimension for each record:

| Code | Meaning | Application |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Documented | Identifiable documentation is present |
| 0 | Omission | Applicable documentation is absent |
| U | Unclear / insufficient information | The available record does not permit a determinate judgment |
| N/A | Not applicable | The dimension is not relevant to the record |

### Important distinctions

- **0 is not the same as U:** use 0 when an applicable element is observably absent; use U when the available information is insufficient to determine whether it is present.
- **U is not the same as N/A:** use U when assessment is uncertain; use N/A when the dimension genuinely does not apply.
- Do not infer undocumented investigative work. The audit evaluates what is visible in the benchmark record.
- Do not treat the existence of a source citation as proof that the evidence supports the verdict.

## Interpretation

The six dimensions are complementary. A record may have strong source linkage but weak contextual coverage, or acknowledge uncertainty without addressing counterevidence.

The rubric evaluates the inspectability of the documented verification process, not factual correctness alone.

## Annotation model

All 120 records in this study were annotated using Claude Sonnet 5.5. This is a model-assisted annotation exercise, not an independently replicated human inter-annotator study. The results should therefore be interpreted with that limitation in mind.
